01 / THE BIG IDEAYOUNG CITY · ANCIENT LAND

Start with a contradiction

The city is young.
The story isn't.

Navoi was founded in 1958. But the region around it carries traces of people, trade and belief from long before the city had a name.

Think of Navoi as a modern base camp for exploring a much older world: prehistoric engravings in the hills, a royal Silk Road stop in the steppe, sacred springs and a vast lake under an enormous sky.

05 / PEOPLE & NAMESLEGACY ISN'T ONLY ABOUT STONES

Names that travel

From a poet's name
to new voices.

The city honours a literary giant from another century — while the region continues to produce athletes, musicians and artists of its own.

A / THE NAMESAKE
1441—1501

Alisher Navoi

Poet, thinker and statesman. Born in Herat, he became a defining figure of Uzbek literature. The city and region carry his name — he did not live in the modern city, founded centuries later.

B / SPORT
BORN IN NAVOI REGION

Khusniddin Norbekov

Uzbek para-athlete, Paralympic champion and world champion in para-athletics. His career puts the region on an international sporting map.

C / MUSIC
BORN IN NAVOI CITY

Dilshod Narzillaev

Uzbek cellist who has performed internationally — a reminder that the region’s identity is not only industrial.

Name note: Alisher Navoi is the historical namesake; Khusniddin Norbekov is from the wider region; Dilshod Narzillaev was born in Navoi city.

07 / DETAILS THAT STAY WITH YOUFIELD NOTES

Look a little closer

The details make
the place.

Navoi is more than a stop between famous cities. Its character becomes clearer in the small connections between landscape, engineering, culture and daily life.

01 / WATER

In the desert, water is architecture.

Malik Sardoba, near Rabat-i Malik, was built to collect and protect water for travellers. Along old trade routes, a dependable water supply could matter as much as shelter.

02 / A CHANGED LANDSCAPE

A lake with an unusual origin.

Aydarkul grew after water from the irrigation system flooded low-lying areas in 1969. Today, its blue horizon feels almost unreal against the Kyzylkum landscape.

03 / LIVING HERITAGE

Tradition is still in the details.

Brick patterns, calligraphy, courtyard spaces and local hospitality connect the region’s historic monuments with a wider Uzbek cultural tradition.

✳

A final thought
Navoi is a reminder that a place does not need to be ancient to have a deep story — sometimes the most interesting part is how the new and the old meet.
